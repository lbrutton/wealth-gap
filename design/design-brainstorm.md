# Wealth Gap Game: Brainstorm

## v1 revisions (supersede v0 below where they conflict)

**Parked:** Heat (exposure meter). Treat all Heat references below as on hold. Exposure applies only to the hunter cabal: it can't be captured, but if exposed it is lost.

**Framing:** world map view. Player turn: pick countries/institutions, enact policies. Then the top 10 billionaires each take a turn.

**Player:** a shadow organization, not a country. Each turn you spend Influence to take control of institutions and use their actions.

**Board = institutions.** Countries, religions, unions, media outlets, central banks, hunter cabals, tax havens. The player represents the people/the world and can embody any non-captured institution each turn.

**Asymmetry: billionaires never control institutions directly.** They act through money:
- Each institution has a **Capture** meter. Billionaire actions (lobbying, asset buying, party funding, media ownership) raise it.
- Capture creates friction before lockout: policies there cost more Influence, some get blocked or watered down, and repeals can be forced between your turns.
- At 100% Capture the institution is **lost**: you can no longer embody it. It becomes a permanent tool for them (a haven, a hostile government, a propaganda outlet).
- Reclaiming a lost institution only through drastic means (revolution, collapse, mass conversion), at high Heat.
- Endgame pressure comes from the map shrinking: fewer institutions you can embody means fewer levers each turn. This is the vicious cycle made visible.

**Opponents:** the 100 richest people on earth.
- Too many to act individually. The top ~10 are named characters with their own agendas and turns. Ranks 11-100 are a passive "r > g" drift plus occasional events.
- The list is ranked and refills. Kill or ruin #7 and #101 joins the top 100. Only total share matters, so removing individuals helps less than expected. New entrants come from whatever is booming (AI founders if automation is unchecked).
- Use parody characters, not the real top 100. Assassination and divorce mechanics against real named people carry defamation and right-of-publicity risk, and could get the game pulled from stores.

**End conditions:** top 100's share of global wealth.
- Reality check: global household wealth ~$470T, top 100 ~$7-9T, so they start around 1.5-2%. Under 1% (win) is only a halving, while 50% (loss) is 25-30x growth. Options: (a) rescale to start ~2%, lose at ~10%, win at ~0.5%; (b) measure a "Power" score (wealth + institutions controlled) instead of raw wealth; (c) start the game in a fictional near-future where they already hold 15-20%.

**Turn structure:** your phase (spend Influence/Funds, take actions via controlled institutions) -> their phase (top 10 each act, shown as a news feed) -> world update (wealth compounds, elections, events).

**Your resources:**
- Influence: take/hold control of institutions
- Funds: from institutions you control (taxes from countries, tithes from religions, stolen assets from the cabal)
- Heat: how exposed your organization is. Covert and drastic actions raise it. Max Heat = you're found and dismantled (third loss condition). Replaces Legitimacy.

**Conversion ("nice billionaires"):** converted billionaires donate a % of returns each turn (Giving Pledge mechanic) and can relapse. Routes: religion, divorce (splits wealth; the ex may be convertible, MacKenzie Scott archetype), heir radicalization, near-death experiences.

**Billionaire actions:** buy assets in a country (raises their control there), fund far-right movements (lower your Influence in that country), stage false-flag attacks (spike security budgets, raise your Heat), union busting, offshoring, lobbying for automation, buying media, funding think tanks that discredit your institutions.

**New levers:**
- Engineered plague: kills working-age population, wages spike (Black Death precedent). Massive Heat.
- Slow automation: lowers returns to capital, starves tech billionaires and stops new entrants, but slows global growth.

## Pitch
Plague Inc in reverse. The pathogen is capital concentration. You are the state, trying to keep the country livable for as long as possible before oligarchy (or collapse) ends the run.

## Theoretical spine
- **r > g (Piketty):** wealth compounds automatically every turn at the return rate. Doing nothing = losing. Your job is to raise g (wages, growth) and lower r (taxing returns, breaking monopolies).
- **The Great Leveler (Walter Scheidel):** historically only four things have reduced inequality significantly: mass-mobilization war, revolution, state collapse, plague. That gives you the dark tier of the game for free, and the joke/thesis: the effective levers are all horrific.

## Two ways to lose (the core tension)
1. **Oligarchy:** top 1% wealth share crosses a threshold. They own the media, the parliament, your debt. Game over.
2. **Collapse:** you push too hard. Capital flight, bond market revolt, sanctions, coup, or your own enforcers become the new elite.

Win condition = survive as many years as possible. Score = years survived x people lifted out of poverty.

## Resources
| Resource | Earned by | Spent on |
|---|---|---|
| **Solidarity** (Plague Inc "DNA points") | People leaving poverty (pop bubbles on the map) | Unlocking policies and institutions |
| **Legitimacy** | Elections, popular policies, visible wins | Drastic actions burn it fast |
| **Authority** (state capacity) | Funding tax agency, courts, police | Enforcing anything. A wealth tax with no tax agency collects nothing |
| **Treasury / Debt** | Taxes | Programs. Who holds your debt matters: if the rich do, they can squeeze you |

## The opposition (the "plague")
- A roster of ~6-10 oligarchs with traits (Media Baron, Landlord King, Tech Monopolist, Energy Dynasty, Hedge Fund, Old Money Heir). Parody names, not real people.
- They spread across **sectors**, not regions: media, housing, politics, energy, food, finance. Each captured sector grants a power:
  - Media: drains your Legitimacy each turn
  - Politics: blocks or slows legislation
  - Housing: rent extraction, pushes people back into poverty
  - Finance: can trigger bond sell-offs on your debt
- They evolve countermeasures as you act (Plague Inc's cure bar, inverted): offshoring, think tanks, union busting, funding the opposition party, lawsuits, buying out your allies.

## Vicious cycle mechanic
Wealth -> ownership -> political influence -> policy rollback -> more wealth. Visualized as a single "Capture" meter feeding back into their growth rate. Each sector captured makes the next cheaper.

## Elections
Every N turns. If you lose, the opposition (funded by them) reverses a random set of your policies. Prevents "set and forget." Forces you to keep Legitimacy up, which discourages pure dark-tier play.

## Lever tiers
**Tier 1: Policy** (always available)
Income tax brackets, capital gains, inheritance, wealth tax, minimum wage, public housing, education, healthcare, UBI.

**Tier 2: Institutions** (Solidarity unlocks)
Unions (key, and the primary target of the rich), antitrust, tax agency funding, public media, campaign finance limits, co-op incentives. Unions are units on the map that can be attacked/infiltrated and need defending.

**Tier 3: International** (world map layer)
Tax havens are other countries. Sign tax treaties, form blocs, sanction havens, capital controls. Alliances make offshoring harder. Havens counter-lobby. Other countries can retaliate against you.

**Tier 4: Drastic** (high Authority, heavy Legitimacy cost)
Nationalization, asset seizure, exile, show trials, unexplained yacht sinkings, state religion that demonizes wealth, the guillotine. Satirical tone.
- Each one triggers capital flight, sanctions, martyrdom (heirs inherit with a vengeance bonus).
- **Nomenklatura mechanic:** the Authority you build to do this creates a new elite of party officials/generals who start accumulating wealth themselves. Concentrate power to fight concentrated wealth, and you become the new plague. Animal Farm ending.

**Tier 5: Great Leveler events**
- War: kills workforce, wages spike (labor scarcity), destroys capital. Huge Legitimacy and population cost.
- Revolution: reset button, random outcome (could install a worse elite).
- Plague: random event, or... not random.

## Country selection
Real starting data from World Inequality Database (wid.world): top 1%/10% shares, Gini, union density, tax rates, debt-to-GDP, offshore wealth estimates (Zucman / EU Tax Observatory). Each country is a difficulty level:
- Sweden: low Gini, high billionaire density, strong unions. "Easy" but hidden trap.
- US: high capture, weak unions, dollar lets you borrow a lot.
- Russia: oligarchs already own everything. Hard mode.
- South Africa / Brazil: extreme gap, high Legitimacy potential.
- Small island nation: you are the tax haven. Weird mode.

## Complexity target
Closer to Plague Inc / Democracy 4 than Civ. Turn-based (quarters), ~20-40 turn runs, one screen with a map + policy web.
Closest existing references: **Democracy 4** (policy web, voter groups), **Plague Inc** (spread, points, cure race), **Suzerain** (narrative political choices), **Tropico** (satirical tone).

## Open questions
- Tone: dark satire (Plague Inc / Tropico) or earnest sim?
- Single country map with sector spread, or world map with countries as havens?
- Turn-based or real-time with pause?
- Platform: browser (fast to prototype, shareable) vs Godot/Unity.

## Suggested MVP
One country, 4 resources, ~15 levers, 6 oligarchs, elections, both lose conditions. Browser, turn-based. Goal: find out if the r > g pressure + election cycle is fun before building tiers 3-5.

## v2: Action economy (proposal)
Mirror economies, both snowball:
- **Player:** base 3 actions + 1 per X million people lifted out of poverty since last turn (Plague Inc bubble pops). Flow, not stock: if poverty creeps back, actions drop. Unspent points bank, capped.
- **Billionaires:** each of the top 10 gets 1 base action + 1 per $Y00B wealth (or +1 per fully captured country). Richer = more actions = richer. The vicious cycle lives in the action count.
- **Costs per action vary by institution:** tax hike in a low-capture country is cheap, same hike in a 70%-captured one costs 3. Capture is felt as action cost, not just a meter.
- **Dampener needed:** both sides snowball, so rising cost per extra action (1st cheap, 5th expensive) or a hard cap, to stop runs being decided by turn 5.
